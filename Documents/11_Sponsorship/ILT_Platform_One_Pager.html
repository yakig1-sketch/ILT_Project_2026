Open-source research platform · Bar-Ilan University

ILT: spotting literacy difficulties early, from how a child learns to write a new letter

The Invented Letter Task (ILT) Platform turns a validated paper-and-digitizer research test into a digital system that schools, research labs and clinics can run on pen tablets, free of licence fees.

The needChildren who struggle are found too late

Dyslexia, dysgraphia and reading-comprehension difficulties are usually identified only after a child has already fallen behind. Children from low socio-economic backgrounds are at the highest risk of going unnoticed.

Research by Dr. Hagit Hollander and Prof. Esther Adi-Japha shows that how well a second-grader learns and retains a new handwriting movement predicts their reading and writing at the end of the school year. A test of that learning can flag children at risk months before formal failure.

How it worksA child writes one invented letter, again and again

On a pen tablet, the child writes a letter they have never seen: one continuous stroke through three dots, in a grid of 15 boxes. Because the letter is new, the test measures pure motor learning, not prior knowledge or home language.

  1. Day 1Learning: speed and accuracy across practice blocks
  2. Day 2Overnight consolidation of the new skill
  3. Day 14Retention without further practice

An evaluator follows each stroke live on a laptop. Each block is scored automatically when it ends, and every session can be replayed stroke by stroke.

Built for researchRaw data first, privacy by design

  • Every pen sample is kept, so scores can be recomputed when the scoring method improves.
  • No names or identity data are stored. Analysts see only de-identified research data.
  • Hebrew and English with full right-to-left support; new languages need no code changes.
  • Fully open source, built only on open-source components.
